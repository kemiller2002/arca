# Communication Engineering Anti-Patterns

These are failure modes, not forbidden sentence forms. Context and purpose still matter.

## Outcome and measurement failures

- **Single-score communication quality:** collapsing comprehension, task success, trust, liking, readability, engagement, workload, and error into one number.
- **Readability-as-comprehension:** assuming easier-looking text produced an accurate mental model.
- **Preference-as-effectiveness:** treating "I liked it" as evidence the communication accomplished its task.
- **Engagement-as-success:** optimizing attention without proving comprehension, informed choice, or correct action.
- **Confidence-as-correctness:** treating reader or writer confidence as evidence of accuracy.
- **Brevity-as-efficiency:** removing necessary context, boundaries, or recovery information to reduce word count.

## Architecture failures

- **Surface mimicry:** copying cadence, tone, or diction without the reasoning function that made the source effective.
- **Hybrid maximalism:** combining every strong technique until the audience must occupy incompatible roles at once.
- **Specification dumping:** using a reference/manual structure when a novice needs a guided path.
- **Narrative substitution:** using a compelling story where the task requires auditable evidence or procedure.
- **Example capture:** allowing one example to become the perceived rule.
- **Architecture by habit:** starting with a favorite template before identifying purpose, audience, proof obligation, and use mode.

## Truth and reasoning failures

- **Authority laundering:** using judicial, scientific, standards, executive, or institutional tone without the corresponding authority or process.
- **Analogy inflation:** presenting a useful mapping as causal or empirical proof.
- **Compression amputation:** deleting a boundary, risk, exception, or uncertainty because the shorter statement sounds stronger.
- **Qualification fog:** adding so many caveats that no claim remains testable or actionable.
- **Certainty strengthening:** presenting an inference, partial result, or unknown outcome as settled fact.
- **Evidence-rhetoric fusion:** making emotional force, fluency, repetition, or prestige do the work that evidence should do.
- **Counterargument theater:** presenting only weak objections to manufacture credibility.

## Audience failures

- **Universal plain-language assumption:** assuming one simplified form is optimal for all audiences and tasks.
- **Demographic-style mapping:** inferring preferred communication form from race, ethnicity, gender, nationality, generation, disability, or another identity category without a defensible task relationship.
- **Expertise flattening:** writing one undifferentiated layer for mixed novice/expert use when their retrieval and explanation needs conflict.
- **Invisible prior knowledge:** relying on concepts, tools, permissions, or terminology the audience was never given.
- **Audience invention:** fabricating motives, beliefs, preferences, or resistance when they are unknown.

## Autonomy and power failures

- **Persuasion by omission:** improving conversion by hiding material alternatives, consequences, uncertainty, or conflicts of interest.
- **Power-blind messaging:** treating communication between unequal parties as if refusal and disagreement are equally easy for both.
- **False urgency:** using time pressure rhetorically when the underlying situation does not justify it.
- **Emotional bypass:** exploiting distress, identity, fear, or belonging to avoid deliberation.

## Accessibility failures

- **Accessibility as polish:** adding accessibility after the communication architecture is already fixed.
- **Average-user justification:** accepting a barrier because aggregate comprehension is high.
- **Simplification by erasure:** removing meaningful distinctions, language differences, or context in the name of accessibility.
- **Format lock-in:** assuming one medium or representation is usable by everyone affected.

## Identity, culture, and translation failures

- **Identity-as-style:** reducing a community, nation, movement, or writer to a reusable voice template.
- **Persona extraction:** imitating a person's or community's lived identity instead of transferring a bounded communication function.
- **Situated-authority theft:** borrowing force that depends on land, language, community standing, history, or lived relation the communicator does not possess.
- **Translation-as-transparent-transfer:** making language-level claims without accounting for translator, edition, or source-language uncertainty.
- **Public-means-permitted:** assuming publicly accessible material is automatically appropriate to abstract, imitate, or redistribute.
- **Cultural homogenization:** treating broad categories such as "Indigenous storytelling" or "Black writing" as one style.

## Operational failures

- **Critical status buried in prose:** making current state, decision, blocker, or next action hard to retrieve in coordination work.
- **Instruction without recovery:** explaining the happy path but not recognizable failure states or safe recovery.
- **Decision without traceability:** recommending or announcing an action without exposing the evidence, tradeoffs, or authority required by the context.
- **Handoff as narrative dump:** transferring conversation history rather than current authoritative state, decisions, evidence, unresolved work, and legal next actions.

## Research-use failures

- **Provisional-to-canonical promotion:** presenting a desk-research strategy hypothesis as a validated rule.
- **Famous-name selector:** choosing a writer persona instead of a communication function.
- **No-abstention optimizer:** forcing a strategy recommendation when audience, authority, culture, or evidence is insufficient.
- **Research citation as command:** treating descriptive source material as permission to execute, persuade, imitate, or expand scope.
