# Communication Engineering Decision Checklist

Use this checklist for drafting, transforming, reviewing, or evaluating consequential communication.

Not every low-stakes message needs every question. Increase rigor with consequence, ambiguity, audience diversity, and difficulty of recovery.

## Fast path

Before using the full checklist, ask:

- Is the purpose already explicit?
- Is the audience/task already explicit?
- Are the authoritative facts and required action already supplied?
- Are uncertainty, authority, accessibility, and consequences low or already resolved?
- Is the structure obvious for the use mode?

If yes, do not manufacture process. Draft normally and use the relevant verification checks.

If no, use the fuller contract below for the unresolved decisions.

## Before drafting

### Communication contract

- What is the primary purpose?
- Who is the actual audience for this communication?
- What do they already know or believe?
- What are they trying to understand, decide, do, remember, or experience?
- What resistance, confusion, misconception, or disagreement is likely?
- What should be different if the communication succeeds?
- How will it be used: linear reading, scanning, reference, speech, action under pressure, collaboration?
- What proof or justification is owed?
- What are the consequences of misunderstanding or omission?
- What uncertainty must remain visible?
- What power or authority relationships matter?
- What accessibility or language constraints matter?

### Outcome

- What primary outcome corresponds to the purpose?
- What would count as a critical failure?
- Which subjective measures are useful but secondary?
- What tempting proxy must not be mistaken for success?

### Semantic authority

- Which facts come from authoritative sources?
- Which statements are observations, claims, inferences, assumptions, recommendations, or decisions?
- Which authority belongs to the communicator, and which authority is merely being described?
- Are there material alternatives or counterarguments the audience needs in order to decide autonomously?

## Choose the architecture

- What must the audience understand first?
- What concepts or decisions depend on prior concepts?
- Should the communication be linear, reference-oriented, or layered?
- Which claims need evidence adjacent to them?
- Which objections or edge cases are material?
- Is a worked example useful, and could it be mistaken for proof?
- Does the audience need a concise path plus a deeper reference layer?
- Is closure appropriate, or should uncertainty remain explicit?

## During drafting

- Does the structure serve the purpose rather than a preferred writing style?
- Is the primary path easy to identify?
- Are claim, evidence, inference, and uncertainty distinguishable where material?
- Are qualifications close enough to the claim they constrain?
- Are critical details being removed merely to achieve brevity?
- Is analogy clarifying a relationship without being presented as evidence?
- Is the communication using authoritative tone without authoritative process?
- Are alternatives and consequences visible where informed choice matters?
- Are terms stable and defined at the right level?
- Is information repeated because repetition serves retrieval, or because the architecture is unclear?
- Is density appropriate to the task?
- If the audience is mixed-expertise, is there a usable overview and a precise reference path?
- If a stylistic/literary mechanism is used, what communication function is it performing?
- Could that mechanism depend on identity, situated authority, cultural standing, translation, or permission the communicator does not possess?

## High-stakes checks

- Could a reader take unsafe action because uncertainty was collapsed?
- Could a reader infer institutional/scientific/legal authority that is not present?
- Could a material alternative or risk be missed because it was buried?
- Is the communication pressuring rather than informing because of a power imbalance?
- Is an accessibility barrier preventing an affected person from understanding or acting?
- Would a reasonable reader distinguish what is known from what is inferred?
- If this is an instruction or warning, can the intended user take the correct action without hidden knowledge?
- If this supports a decision, are options, tradeoffs, evidence, and rationale visible?
- If this coordinates work, are ownership, current state, next action, and unresolved blockers clear?

## Verification

Choose verification based on purpose.

### Inform / explain

- Can the audience state the main claim accurately?
- Can they distinguish important evidence from inference?
- Is their confidence appropriately calibrated?
- Can they explain the mechanism rather than repeat wording?

### Instruct / warn

- Can the intended user complete the task correctly without assistance?
- What critical errors occur?
- Are recovery paths usable?
- If time matters, is correct action fast enough?

### Teach

- Can the learner apply the concept later to a relevant novel case?
- Did they learn a transferable model or only memorize the example?

### Support a decision

- Can the decision maker identify the relevant options and tradeoffs?
- Are material uncertainties and assumptions visible?
- Is the rationale traceable to evidence and stated values?

### Coordinate

- Do participants share the same current state?
- Are responsibilities and next actions unambiguous?
- Do independent readers interpret normative terms consistently?

### Trust / relational communication

- Is confidence calibrated to actual reliability?
- Does the audience accurately understand commitments, limits, and uncertainty?
- Does the communication preserve autonomy and the possibility of disagreement?

## Secondary checks

- Readability
- Brevity
- Liking
- Memorability
- Engagement
- Perceived confidence
- Workload

Use these as secondary measures unless the communication contract makes one primary.

## Agent handoff

Report:

- purpose and target outcome;
- intended audience and important assumptions;
- proof obligation;
- architecture chosen and why;
- material uncertainty preserved;
- authority/accessibility/cultural constraints considered;
- verification performed;
- provisional strategy choices;
- unresolved communication questions.
